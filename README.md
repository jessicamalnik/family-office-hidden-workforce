# Family office hidden workforce

A public research package mapping the roles, career paths, organizational functions, and trust-based hiring dynamics inside single-family offices, built from the documentary *I Investigated the Secret Industry That Manages Billionaires' Money*.

## What this repository contains

This repository turns the documentary into a structured reference for journalists, researchers, family-office professionals, candidates, and anyone trying to understand how ultra-wealthy families build private organizations around their money and lives.

The core files include:

- `datasets/family-office-roles.csv` — 18 roles and functions named or directly supported by the documentary.
- `datasets/career-proving-grounds.csv` — The institutions and career paths that commonly feed talent into family offices.
- `datasets/family-office-functions.csv` — The operating functions handled inside a family office.
- `datasets/hiring-dynamics.csv` — The trust, referral, and confidentiality patterns shaping hiring.
- `datasets/key-claims.csv` — Statistics and major claims from the documentary with verification status.
- `datasets/family-office-landscape.json` — Machine-readable version of the structured research.
- `source-ledger/sources.md` — Claim-by-claim evidence ledger.
- `research-page/research-page.md` — Long-form canonical research page draft.
- `research-page/seo-metadata.md` — SEO title, description, schema notes, and target queries.

## Core finding

Family offices are often described as wealth-management organizations. That description is incomplete.

The documentary found that many operate more like small private companies built around one family. Their teams may coordinate investments, taxes, legal work, properties, travel, security, philanthropy, technology, cybersecurity, succession, and next-generation education.

The defining operating principle is not simply money.

It is trust.

---

## What is a family office?

A family office is a private organization created to manage the financial and personal affairs of a wealthy family.

At the highest levels of wealth, a family may stop outsourcing every function to banks, accounting firms, law firms, advisers, and outside vendors. Instead, it may begin hiring an internal team.

That team can include investment professionals, controllers, tax directors, chiefs of staff, property managers, security specialists, philanthropy leaders, technology professionals, and family-governance advisers.

---

## Why the industry is difficult to see

Family offices often operate with unusually high levels of confidentiality.

The documentary identifies several recurring hiring dynamics:

- Some organizations hire primarily through word of mouth.
- Recruiters may conceal the client's identity until trust has been established.
- Jobs may never appear on public job boards.
- Candidates are often hired from institutions that have already tested their judgment and discretion.
- Long-standing advisers may be recruited directly by the families they have served.

This creates a hidden relationship economy in which reputation and introductions can matter more than public applications.

## Career proving grounds

The documentary repeatedly identifies the following institutions and professions as common proving grounds:

- Goldman Sachs.
- Morgan Stanley.
- Big Four accounting firms.
- Private equity.
- Investment banking.
- Private banking.
- Estate law.

These organizations are often not the final destination. They are where professionals build the credibility, discretion, and relationships that can eventually lead into family-office work.

## Scale of the industry

The documentary cites an estimate of roughly 8,000 single-family offices worldwide.

It also describes the direct global workforce as numbering in the tens of thousands, while emphasizing that individual specialties can be dramatically smaller.

The documentary further estimates that only a few hundred family-governance specialists may exist worldwide.

These figures require external source verification before publication as independently sourced facts. They are preserved here because they appear in the documentary.

## Methodology

This repository was built exclusively from the documentary transcript supplied by Jessica Malnik.

No outside research was added.

The data reflects:

- Roles explicitly named in the documentary.
- Functions directly described in the documentary.
- Career paths and institutions explicitly named.
- Claims and estimates stated in the documentary.
- Analytical conclusions presented by the documentary.

This is not an exhaustive census of the family-office industry.
